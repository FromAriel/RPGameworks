## W3.2 geometry from the skin, not from `TILE = 16` — November 9, 2026, local

The W1 compositor now takes every size from the skin ([decision 0011](decisions/0011-reusable-window-skins.md) W3.2). **Ariel's skin draws exactly as before**; any skin that passes the v2 reader can now have thin or thick bands, chunky corners, wide title pieces and even-spaced rails.

- **Code.** `layout.ts` has no tile constant: `skinGeometry(skin)` resolves the skin once (corners, band per side, title pieces, rails, medallions, divider pieces, fill, minimum frame size), `spanParts(length, period, spacing)` splits a rail by its own tile length in either spacing mode, and `frameGeometry(geometry, width, height, title)` places the title gap and medallions. `paint.ts` reads the manifest through `readSkinManifest` (an invalid manifest or an atlas of the wrong size now falls back to plain windows with the existing notice), draws corners at the window's corners over everything else, starts and stops each rail where its corners end, and fills plain gaps with the skin's gap fill (a tiled plain piece, or a stretch of the rail's own first or last row or column). `windowskin.ts` no longer assumes a 192 × 192 atlas or a 16-pixel divider band.
- **Rules carried over from W1 and generalized** (each is identical for Ariel's skin): the interior fill starts at half the band on each side; a medallion sits flush with the outer edge, centred (left and right medallions measured from the lowered top); the title gap fits only if both brackets clear the corners and the frame below the lowered top still has room for the corners plus the title band (48 pixels for Ariel's skin, as before); the top medallion appears only when the title is not in the gap; dividers keep W1's mirrored-end spacing with `divider.h.plain` in gaps, the title's text gap beside their labels, and are centred on half their own height.
- **Golden test** (`tests/unit/skin-golden.test.ts` with `tests/unit/skin-harness.ts`): a test-only canvas stand-in records the compositor's draw calls and rasterizes them. Its reference `tests/fixtures/windowskin-golden.json` was **recorded from the unchanged compositor at `2a66694` before any W3.2 edit**: 14 frames (minimum, odd, remainder, medallion threshold, tall, wide, and six title cases including the header fallback) and 4 dividers. After the rewrite all 18 compose to **identical pixels**. The harness models the canvas; it does not replace the browser checks below.
- **Fixture skins** (`tests/unit/skin-fixtures.test.ts`): a thin 4-pixel band, a thick 24-pixel band, 32-pixel chunky corners on an 8-pixel band, wide title pieces, and even-spaced rails with an edge-stretch gap fill, each composed at 7 sizes (odd and even, from the minimum up to 401 × 257) with and without a title. Every band pixel is covered by exactly one frame piece (no gaps), frame pieces never overlap, corners, medallions and title brackets are drawn whole, pattern tiles are never cut, and only a one-row or one-column gap fill stretches. **A deliberate one-pixel shift of the right rail failed the golden test and all five fixtures**; the code was restored and they pass.
- **Checks run locally:** `npm run validate` passed; `npm run check` passed with **442/442** unit tests (the golden test, the 5 fixtures, and the existing W1 geometry tests moved to the new signatures with unchanged expectations), the external-art guard and the build; `check:test-tags` passed; the canonical audit output is unchanged. **Full browser suite 156/156** in 3.5 minutes. **Seen in the dev server:** the inventory window in Ariel's skin at 2× with the bracketed title, medallions and corners.
- Migration: none. Next: **W3.3 automatic content and title fitting**.

## W3.1 skin manifest format v2 — November 9, 2026, local

A window skin can now describe its own geometry in data ([decision 0011](decisions/0011-reusable-window-skins.md) W3.1, with Ariel's answers recorded there). **Nothing on screen changes:** the compositor still reads the v1 manifest directly until W3.2.

- **Format v2** (`schemas/windowskin.schema.json`, generated validator `src/content/generated/windowskin-validator.mjs`): atlas size; tiles with a rectangle of any size or `"same": "<id>"` to reuse art; band thickness per side; title text centre and text-to-bracket gap (the title band height is the title tiles' height); per rail a spacing mode (`mirrored-end` or `even`) and a gap fill (a plain tile, or a stretch of the rail's own first or last row or column); optional medallions per side with a minimum edge length; `contentMargin` (default 4) and optional exact `contentInsets`; an optional `outset` on a tile, which must be 0 for now.
- **Reader and validator** (`src/presentation/skin/skin-manifest.mjs`, pure, DOM-free): `readSkinManifest` accepts v1 and v2; **a v1 manifest is read as v2 with W1's exact values** (16-pixel bands, text centre 8, 4-pixel bracket gap, mirrored-end rails, plain-tile gap fill except the right edge's `railEdge: "start"`, medallions left, right and bottom on edges of at least 96, no top medallion, the declared insets 16/16/20/16 as overrides). `validateSkinV2` reports, with the tiles and values involved: duplicate ids; a tile with both or neither of rect and same; aliases to missing tiles, to themselves or to other aliases; tiles outside the atlas; overlapping rectangles (with position and size); missing required roles (the 22 roles W1 draws); a band under 1 pixel; rails or gap-fill tiles whose thickness differs from their side's band; corners thinner than the band; unequal title tile heights, a title band thinner than the top band, a text centre outside the band; unequal divider heights; an enabled medallion with no tile, thinner than the band, or with a minimum edge too short for both corners and itself; insets that would put content on the frame; and any outward overhang. `frameReach` and `contentInsets` give the derived numbers.
- **Audit tool generalized** (`tools/audit-windowskin.mjs`): `--manifest <file>` audits any v1 or v2 skin (PNG beside it, noninterlaced RGBA8 matching the declared atlas size, transparent outside every tile); `--write` writes `<name>.audit.json` beside it. Ariel's canonical file still gets W1's strict checks, and **its output is byte-identical to the committed `Window.audit.json`** (checked with `cmp` and by the existing unit test).
- **Checks run locally:** `npm run validate` passed; `npm run check` passed with **436/436** unit tests (7 new in `tests/unit/skin-manifest.test.ts`: Ariel's skin read as v1 and as written-out v2, derived insets and a chunky corner, 17 invalid cases each with its message, structural and version errors, and the audit run on a generated v2 skin, including a stray gutter pixel and a size mismatch), the external-art guard and the build. `test:smoke` passed 13/13 (no runtime change expected). `Window.png` and `Window.manifest.json` are unchanged (their pinned hashes still pass).
- Migration: none. Next: **W3.2 geometry from the skin, not from `TILE = 16`**.

## M5.3a timeline battle kernel — November 8, 2026, local

M5.3 was split in two. **M5.3a** (this packet) replaces the round-based practice kernel with the turn-timeline rules from [decision 0006](decisions/0006-battle-system-direction.md); **M5.3b** will replace the interim dialogue-window presentation with a dedicated side-view battle scene.

**Rules (`src/domain/battle.ts`, pure TypeScript):** every combatant has a next-action tick. The living combatant with the lowest tick acts; ties go to the party, then the lower slot. Acting adds `ceil(1000 × weight / (100 × speed))` ticks, with weights Attack 100, Item 80 and Guard 60, so lighter actions come around sooner. The kernel exposes an 8-turn timeline and `preview(command)`, computed from the same rule without randomness, so the order shown is the order executed until the hero's next decision, apart from combatants who are defeated first. Damage keeps the seeded `max(1, attack − defense ± 1)` stream. Guard halves every hit, rounding up, until the hero acts again. An item heals one living ally, up to full; healing at full HP is refused. Escape always ends an ordinary battle. Enemy turns resolve automatically until the next hero decision; a faster enemy can act before the hero's first turn. `validate` returns a reason (wrong phase, invalid or defeated target, unusable item, full HP, unknown command) without touching state or randomness. Retry is available only after defeat and restores the identical opening; spent items are not refunded.

**Content:** the encounter catalog is now version 2 with `party` (1–4) and `enemies` (1–6), each combatant having an ID, name, battler atlas frame, HP, attack, defense and **speed**. Validation rejects duplicate combatant IDs, blank names and unknown battler frames. Items may declare `battleUse: {type: "heal", amount}`. The demo Practice Sigil encounter is now the hero (30 HP, speed 10) against the Practice Sigil (20 HP, speed 8) and a faster Sigil Shard (10 HP, speed 14). A new Workshop tonic (heals 12, stacks to 9) comes from a new supply crate in the Supply Room that grants two. The new item and placement IDs are additive; per the early-demo policy no migration was needed.

**Interim presentation:** the dialogue window now shows both sides' HP, the resolved events, whose turn it is, and the next six turns ("Next: Hero → Practice Sigil → …"). Commands are one button per living enemy (Attack …), Guard, a tonic button with the carried count (disabled with "HP is already full." when it cannot help) and Escape. After each command the results are shown with Continue. Using a tonic first commits a session transaction that removes one; the battle resolves only if it committed. Cancel continues past results, otherwise escapes. Impact effects still play after resolution. The runtime snapshot's `battle` now reports turn, active combatant, HP by ID and the timeline.

## CA1 character pack v1 foundations — delivered (synthetic pixels only)

The character pack format, deterministic offline encoder, and strict runtime reader exist and are tested end to end against synthetic pixels; no licensed pixels, `.import/` input, or runtime fetch is involved. Recorded as [decision 0003](decisions/0003-character-pack-v1.md), which owns the full wire layout: magic `RPGP`, version byte 1, reserved 0, bounded UTF-8 JSON manifest, then zlib-compressed raw RGBA payloads addressed by deduplicated manifest entries (`payloads` with sequential offsets/lengths/decodedLengths/SHA-256 digests; `cells` binding animation|direction|frameIndex × layer → payload). Every payload decodes to exactly the uniform cell size; fail-closed bounds: manifest ≤ 256 KiB, decoded payload ≤ 128×128 RGBA, total decoded and payload body ≤ 8 MiB, ≤ 65 535 payloads / 65 536 cells / 16 layers / 256 animations / 16 frames per animation, plus ≥1 layer per animation frame with subset layer coverage permitted per frame.

The encoder (`tools/build-character-pack.mjs`, explicit command, never part of routine `npm run build`) validates a JSON pack source through `schemas/character-pack-source.schema.json` plus cross-field rules (duplicate animation/layer IDs, unknown layers, frameIndex beyond declared frameCount, wrong pixel-symbol counts, unknown palette symbols, missing frames), then encodes byte-identically across runs and invariant to source frame order (deflate level 9, canonical walk, no timestamps), writing `<packId>.rpgpack` and a `<packId>.report.json` generation report (source/pack hashes, byte and cell/payload counts) only after full validation, via staging plus rename. Pair replacement is ordered recoverably: the regenerable report (`regenerable: true`, never authoritative — the pack blob is the artifact of record) is replaced first and the pack second; a failing pack replacement surfaces a contextual error and preserves an existing pack while the report remains regenerable; rerunning the command re-derives the pair from scratch. The report records a repository-relative source identifier for in-repo inputs. The runtime reader (`src/platform/character-pack.ts`) verifies framing, strict UTF-8 JSON (schema `schemas/character-pack-manifest.schema.json`, ajv-standalone generated alongside the content validators), cross-field manifest structure (sequential payload offsets, exact payload-body extent, uniform decoded sizes, cell uniqueness, animation coverage, direction consistency, payload index/layer references), and decodes payloads lazily through `DecompressionStream` with a chunk-wise decoded-length cap — a hostile pack declaring a 64-byte payload over a 1 MiB deflate stream is cut off mid-read — and per-payload SHA-256 verification after inflation; corrupt compressed streams surface a clear failure instead of an opaque DOM error. Cancellation is reader-owned: an abort signal interrupts an in-flight read by cancelling the held reader (the `pipeThrough`-locked source is never touched, which previously rejected while discarded), tested for in-flight aborts, abort during digest verification, already-aborted signals, and completed decodes. The reader is data-only: no eval, no paths, no network. `tools/generate-schemas.mjs` now also generates the manifest validator/declarations.
